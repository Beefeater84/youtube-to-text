## The Importance of Resumes in the Age of AI

Finding reliable resume advice online was already hard enough. Now that employers are using AI to screen job applications, it's fair to ask, 'Do resumes even matter anymore?' I spent weeks analyzing hiring reports and academic studies from institutions like MIT and Oxford, covering more than 4,000 hiring managers and nearly 2 million applications. Bottom line up front: resumes still matter, but the rules have changed. In this video, I'll share the five most important findings and what each one means for you. Then, I'll break down exactly what you need to do to stand out in the age of AI.

<!-- t:38 -->
## Key Findings on Resumes and AI

Kicking things off with the five key findings. First, 87% of hiring managers say their AI hiring software reads simple, text-based resumes more accurately than fancy, visual ones. Second, tailored resumes saw 84% higher interview rates, but resumes with the most keywords actually performed worse. Third, 59% of hiring managers see candidates using AI as a good sign, but 28% reject AI-heavy resumes that show little to no effort. Fourth, resumes that quantified a candidate's impact saw 75% higher interview rates than those that only described responsibilities. Fifth, 60% of hiring managers want candidates to prove their AI skills, not just list them.

<!-- t:91 -->
## Two Stages of Resume Evaluation

Before diving into each finding, let's cover the two stages your resume has to pass in this new era: pass the AI and pass the human. Stage one is to pass the AI. Companies use AI to read, rank, and organize applications, so your resume needs to be easy to process and clearly show how your experience matches the role. Stage two is to pass the human. Only 6% of hiring managers say AI is the decision-maker when it comes to passing or rejecting candidates, meaning humans still play a central role in hiring. Your resume needs to take the human reader into account as well.

<!-- t:138 -->
## Rule 1: Make Your Resume AI-Friendly

Rule number one is to make sure the AI can read your resume. While 87% of employers say their AI hiring software prefers simple, text-based resumes, many AI design tools encourage job seekers to use graphics, icons, and large images. We should optimize our resumes for readability, not appearance. Keep the formatting intentionally boring, use one column, and conventional headings like summary, experience, education, and skills. Avoid skill bars. Export your resume as a selectable text PDF, ideally below 2.5 megabytes. A quick check is to open the PDF and see if you can highlight and copy the text.

<!-- t:227 -->
## Rule 2: Tailor Your Resume Effectively

Rule number two is to make your fit obvious. Tailored resumes saw 84% higher interview rates, but resumes with the most keywords performed worse. The difference lies between keyword mapping and keyword stuffing. Keyword mapping uses relevant keywords from the job description to describe actual work done, while keyword stuffing crams in phrases from the job description without backing them up. Studies show untailored resumes had a 3.09% interview rate versus 5.71% for tailored resumes. Using AI to over-optimize can do more harm than good. To tailor your resume faster without keyword stuffing, give AI the job description and your base resume, ask it to identify key problems and skills, and then suggest stronger bullet points.

<!-- t:371 -->
## Rule 3: Use AI Thoughtfully

Rule number three is to know where AI should stop. While 59% of hiring managers like to see candidates using AI, 28% reject AI-heavy resumes that show little effort. Thoughtful AI use is encouraged, while lazy prompts produce generic content that hiring managers are tired of reading. To use AI thoughtfully, first, brain dump your raw thoughts for each work experience, covering the final result, your contribution, and how you achieved it. Then, upload the job description and your notes to AI to strengthen the wording without changing the underlying facts.

<!-- t:518 -->
## Rule 4: Quantify Your Impact

Rule number four is to prove your impact with numbers. Research found resumes that quantified a candidate's impact saw 75% higher interview rates than those that only described responsibilities. Adding metrics to your resume has been best practice for decades. AI makes it easier to identify relevant metrics and polish bullet points. Upload your resume and ask AI to help identify metrics for each experience. Use Google's XYZ formula to rewrite each bullet without changing any facts or numbers.

<!-- t:636 -->
## Rule 5: Demonstrate Your AI Skills

Rule number five is to prove your AI skills. 60% of hiring managers want candidates to demonstrate their AI skills, not just list them. When looking for proof, 26% prefer an interview or task, 19% want work examples, and 15% look for certifications. An Oxford University experiment found that adding role-relevant AI skills increased a candidate's chance of being selected for an interview by up to 15 percentage points. Make the first bullet under each experience an AI achievement relevant to the job description. If you lack workplace examples, complete a small project related to the job and list it under a separate project section.

## Recap and Conclusion

To recap, rules one and two help you pass the AI by making your resume readable and connecting your experience to the role. Rules three, four, and five help you pass the human by using AI thoughtfully, quantifying your impact, and proving your AI skills with real work. While not every finding from this video is new, seeing the data behind the advice can help you follow it with more confidence, especially now that AI is involved on both sides of hiring.

## Introduction to Resume Writing Tips

As a recruiter and a career coach, I look at a lot of résumés and I do a lot of résumé reviews. One thing is perfectly clear: some of you need help. In this video, I'm going to share some résumé writing tips that will help your resume not suck. My approach comes from a corporate recruiter's perspective, how I screen and look at résumés very quickly. We usually give you a five-second glance, but also how hiring managers have given me feedback on how they screen resumes themselves. The feedback and the tips that I teach are from that perspective. I'm not saying that my way is the only way to approach it, but I want to give you some tips that can help you improve your presentation. You have a very short amount of time to capture the attention of the person who is screening the resume.

## Choosing the Right Resume Template

The first thing we want to do is stop using outdated templates designed by graphic designers. These templates are often distracting and do not serve the initial purpose of quickly conveying information. I recommend downloading a very simple template from Microsoft Word. If you want a battle-tested recruiter-approved résumé template, I provide one with Résumé Rocket Fuel, available on my website. When choosing a template, go with one that has one column. Avoid bright and flashy colors and graphics. Make sure the fonts are clean and easy to read. Stick to basic colors like blue, black, or dark green. For most of us, a resume should be relatively boring in that regard. The only exception is if you're a graphic designer or in a creative field where a more creative approach might be expected. However, if you're a graphic designer, using a template designed by another graphic designer is probably not the best look for you. Templates designed by graphic designers often aren't effective because they don't know how applicant tracking systems work and how they parse résumés.

## Contact Information and Resume Customization

Stop making your contact information horizontal. You want to make it vertical because ATS systems typically do not read horizontal formats well. If you find that the applicant tracking system is not parsing your name, phone number, or address cleanly, you probably don't have an ATS-compliant resume. Your contact information isn't usually the reason you aren't getting contacted, but it can speed up your applications. Next, stop writing your resume as a summary of everything you've ever accomplished. Instead, think about profiling for a specific role and delivering exactly what the employer is looking for. This is a much more effective approach. Stop focusing on all the stuff that you've accomplished that probably isn't relevant to the hiring teams and start focusing on what they actually care about.

## The Importance of Customization and Clarity

Customization of your resume is a crucially important step, especially in today's job market, particularly for coveted remote roles. The competition is fierce, so you have to do something to stand out. Customizing your resume is uncomfortable, but you need to get in the habit of doing it. You can't approach your job search like it's 2018; the market is completely different today. While others may not customize their resumes, this is your opportunity to gain a competitive advantage. Additionally, stop being so wordy with your resumes. Long blocks of text can cause recruiters and hiring managers to skip over your resume. Be concise and selective with your words. Remove proprietary language that only you and your coworkers understand. Instead, convey your concepts in clear and common language for the industry you’re applying to.

<!-- t:400 -->
## Avoiding Generic Language and Stubbornness

Remove generic fluffy terminology from your resume. Phrases like 'I'm a team player' or 'I have great organizational skills' do not add value. If those competencies are important, hiring teams will assess them in an interview. Focus on actionable terms and concepts related to the job. The goal is not to impress with elaborate vocabulary but to convey a clear fit for the job. Finally, if your resume isn't working, redo it. Doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results is the definition of insanity. If you're getting frustrated in your job search and facing repeated rejections, your resume may not be working for you. Be realistic about your fit for the jobs you're applying to. Make sure your resume clearly conveys your fit to give yourself the best chance of getting into the interview process.

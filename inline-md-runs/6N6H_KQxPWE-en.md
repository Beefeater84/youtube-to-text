What is happening in the job market right now? It's an interesting question. ## Hiring Volume and Changes in Approaches

When it comes to hiring in general, we had the most fruitful year in 2024. We were bringing in about 35-40 people a month. Now our pace is more modest, around 20 people a month just in IT. Specifically for IT, the typical roles we hire for include backend, tech support, testing, with frontend taking fourth place. Over the last 2-3 years, the approach to hiring has changed significantly. In 2023-2024, we really needed to hire, and we were in a state of strong competition for candidates. There were situations where we revised an offer to a candidate three times in a row because they received a counter-offer. In 2024, this was not an unusual situation.

## Changes in the Job Market and the Impact of AI

In 2023-2024, many companies were actively growing and hiring people. But as agent AI developed, the situation began to change dramatically. Companies started focusing less on hiring people and more on hiring people enhanced by their agents. This leads to even fewer vacancies in the market. Literally on the first day of a vacancy's publication, a recruiter can receive hundreds, sometimes thousands, of applications. But the problem is that even if thousands of resumes are perfect, there is only one vacancy. An employer cannot interview a thousand people. They need to interview a maximum of 15 and select one finalist.

## Using AI in Recruitment

In the recruitment process in HR, if you have more than 300 people, there are always systems where you manage and store candidates. These systems already have built-in resume evaluation systems. When a resume comes in, the ATS system evaluates it based on points or stars. However, the AI Act applies to European candidates, which states that each candidate must be evaluated and decisions must be made only by a live person. Therefore, we have disabled AI screening. We have an assistant for analyzing applications. We specify that we would like this candidate to have certain keywords and experience with specific technologies. Each recruiter decides for themselves whether they need AI assistance or not.

## Conclusion and Tips for Job Seekers

First of all, you need to analyze the job postings you are applying to. Upload the job posting and your resume into the AI system and check if there are keywords from the job posting in your resume. Don't try to trick the system by adding keywords in white font. Usually, four to five targeted resumes tailored to the specific requirements of your profession will suffice. However, there are always hidden requirements that will not be published in the job posting. So, when you receive a rejection, don't think it's about you. It's about how the market is structured and the employers' requirements.

## Problems with AI in Recruiting

Well, there's a header in the description about the company as a whole, about some tasks, some disclaimers, and so on. Sometimes the system can't focus on what's important and what's not. A simple solution that seems to have solved about half of the problems is an internal field where the recruiter writes the top critical things that need to be checked. In a large percentage of cases, I see that there are results. We have practically completely abandoned the screening question when you apply as a candidate; you have forms to fill out. Previously, these were key helpers for recruiters, especially during mass hiring when there are many applications, and it's hard to read through resumes; filters don't always work. So, we would say, 'Please, at least based on the form.' I ask, 'Do you have experience with the B2C market or only with B2B, for example? Do you know English at a certain level?' And for these questions, they set an automatic rejection when such forms are used.

## Job Search Strategy

The second story is that I would focus on the fact that there are mass applications, just like mass hiring, and they need to be done fundamentally, while in your head, it's great to build a strategy about which companies you want to target. If you have a dream company working in ecology, for example, or in well-known IT companies that create great products you like, or in medicine, you should spend time studying them and can create a separate resume tailored to their job openings. Not that it has to be completely separate, but just read their key words and adjust them in your resume. Why? Because you will pass the first filter of the same system, and plus, the person looking at it will read that story. Secondly, your task is to get to the next interview. Therefore, the better your resume fits the job opening, the better the outcome will be.

## Cover Letters and Personal Messages

So, thank you for asking. This is the second part of the story. Cover letters, and I would also include the advice to write on LinkedIn, in messengers, to look for recruiters, managers, and send them all your letters. Cover letters are not easy. If it’s a dream company and it’s a heartfelt, great message from you, then yes, write it. But remember that there’s a 99% chance your cover letter won't be read. However, there’s a 1% chance it will be read by a recruiter, you might touch their heartstrings, and then it will go smoothly. So, this can work, but don’t spend hours of your life on it; don’t write them for every job opening because most of the time they are not read. Sometimes they are included in job postings simply because the recruiter forgot to uncheck the box that is pre-set in ATS systems to attach a cover letter.
